# Legal policy baseline

Use this reference when maintaining Hypertext Studio’s website and product policies. Policies must describe the shipped capability and its actual providers. Shared language establishes consistent operator identity and user protections; it is not evidence that every product has the same architecture. Each repository keeps native, independently published policy pages. Do not introduce a shared runtime dependency for legal text.

## Operator and contact

Use **Hypertext Studio** as the operator. Publish **1810 East Sahara Avenue STE 75246, Las Vegas, NV 89104, United States** as the mailing address. Use **legal@hypertext.studio** for official legal notices and privacy requests. Use **contact@hypertext.studio** for general Studio enquiries and **support@hypertext.studio** for product help, billing, security, and abuse reports. Keep **hello@hypertext.studio** as a legacy inbound alias for the general contact address.

Name the covered website, application, and hosted services. Product notices cover their own capabilities; the Studio website notice covers visits, email, the local enquiry composer, and public webmentions. A local-first product claim must not conceal account, billing, support, security, sync, integration, or AI processing.

## Inbound email roles

Publish the canonical Studio addresses below on the Studio website. Product copy may publish verified product-domain `contact@` and `support@` aliases that route to the corresponding canonical Studio destinations. Continue to publish **legal@hypertext.studio** for official legal notices and privacy requests. Apply the same inbound role mapping on Studio-managed product domains where the Studio manages email for that domain. A product domain name does not establish that its mail service or aliases exist; verify the approved domains and actual routes before publication.

| Inbound role on a Studio or product domain    | Canonical Studio destination |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| `contact@`, `hello@`                          | `contact@hypertext.studio`   |
| `support@`, `billing@`, `security@`, `abuse@` | `support@hypertext.studio`   |
| `legal@`, `privacy@`                          | `legal@hypertext.studio`     |

For general product questions and product assistance, publish either the verified product-domain contact/support alias or its canonical Studio destination. For official notices or privacy rights, publish the Studio legal address. Preserve exact historical policy text even when it contains a legacy address. Retain legacy inbound aliases so existing correspondence can reach the appropriate role.

These are inbound addresses and routing targets. They are separate from outbound transactional sender identities, delivery providers, and reply handling. Do not replace an application's `no-reply@` or `noreply@` sender as part of this mapping. Review outbound sender verification, authentication, deliverability, and reply routing separately when that work is authorized. Source references do not establish provider provisioning, delivery, or send-as authorization.

## Privacy notice structure and common prose

Use these sections in this order. A product may add relevant detail within a section, including a provider table or retention schedule, and may split a substantial topic into a clearly linked subsection.

1. **Operator and scope.** “Hypertext Studio operates [product or website]. This notice explains information handled when you use [covered services].” Distinguish the website, installed client, hosted backend, and third-party integrations.
2. **Data and purposes.** Name each data category and the reason for collecting it. Include account, payment, support, security, and diagnostics only where applicable. State the legal basis where required; do not describe every activity as consent-based. Explain legitimate interests in delivery, security, responding to enquiries, relevant public features, and website performance measurement where permitted, balanced against people’s rights; requested pre-contract steps and contract performance for agreed work; and legal obligations for required records or disclosures. Use consent for optional processing only where appropriate or required. Keep consent-required processing disabled until a valid consent mechanism obtains it; optional analytics are not universally consent-based.
3. **Local and hosted processing.** State which information stays on the device, which leaves it, and who can read it. Separate encryption in transit, encryption at rest, and end-to-end encryption. Describe recovery limits accurately.
4. **Recipients and AI.** Identify actual providers and their roles. Explain which information an AI feature sends, its trigger, and relevant provider retention or training terms. “We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising” is appropriate only when verified. Do not promise no third-party access while using hosted services.
5. **Retention and deletion.** Explain periods or criteria by category, deletion routes, and necessary legal, security, backup, or dispute exceptions. Do not invent durations, instantaneous deletion, or provider configuration. Explain the effect of deletion on shared or public content.
6. **Security.** Describe applicable safeguards without guaranteeing complete security. Avoid public credential inventories or sensitive implementation detail.
7. **Choices and rights.** Explain relevant product controls and access, correction, deletion, portability, restriction, objection, consent withdrawal, and complaint rights where applicable. Provide an accessible recovery route. Aggregated analytics do not eliminate rights in other personal information. Honor any stated DNT or GPC promise in the actual collection path.
8. **Children.** Describe the actual audience and handling of children’s information. Product account eligibility and adult payment authority belong in the terms; they do not establish that every general-audience website visitor must be 16.
9. **International processing.** Identify relevant countries or provider locations and applicable safeguards without assuming every provider uses the same transfer arrangement.
10. **Changes and contact.** Give publication and effective dates, an accessible policy archive, notice of material changes where required, and the official contact. “A privacy notice explains processing; continued use alone does not supply consent for a new use that requires your consent.” Do not turn notice revisions into blanket acceptance.

Public webmentions are relevant to the Studio website and may have a dedicated subsection. Explain the public API, source and target URLs, extracted author and content information, hosted storage, generated pages and caches, and removal requests. The enquiry composer prepares a browser-local draft, opens a mail app, or copies to the clipboard; it does not submit a form to the Studio.

## Terms structure and common prose

Use these eleven sections in this order. Keep existing liability amounts and open-source rights unless a separately approved change requires otherwise. Do not introduce arbitration, indemnities, or new use restrictions through this alignment.

For the Studio website, these sections cover the free public pages, local enquiry composer, and public webmentions. Account, payment, and paid-service obligations belong to the applicable product terms or a separately agreed services contract. Keep website content permission tied to the chosen features and preserve existing open-source grants and mandatory rights.

### 1. Operator and scope

“These terms govern your use of [product and covered services], operated by Hypertext Studio.” Name the distribution channels and any separate third-party terms. Explain how the agreement is accepted at the relevant account or purchase step.

### 2. Eligibility

“You must be at least 16 to create an account. If you are not legally able to enter this agreement on your own, your parent or guardian must agree where required. A person making a purchase must be an adult with authority to pay.” Apply account conditions only where accounts exist and preserve stricter applicable law.

### 3. Accounts

Describe the actual account model, responsibility for account security, and a support or recovery route. Do not add a registration requirement to an account-free product. Explain any organization or administrator authority that the product actually offers.

### 4. Your content

“You retain ownership of your content. You give Hypertext Studio only the permission needed to operate, secure, support, and deliver the features you choose to use, including processing or transmitting content as those features require.” Explain public sharing and external recipients where applicable. Do not claim ownership or an unrelated marketing or AI-training license.

### 5. Features and connected services

Describe capability limits, authorization, provider dependencies, and relevant consequences. External actions must follow the user’s actual authorization. Local features, sync, and AI require product-specific language. Avoid warranties that every model output or integration is accurate or always available.

### 6. Billing, cancellation, and refunds

Explain prices, renewal, cancellation, and any effect on content or export. Identify the payment channel. App-store purchases follow that store’s applicable purchase and refund process, subject to mandatory consumer rights.

For **Curfew direct purchases**, allow refund requests within 14 days of the latest subscription payment, including the latest renewal. For **Docket and LogDate direct purchases**, do not promise discretionary refunds; correct billing errors and honor refunds required by law. Do not carry Curfew’s discretionary refund window into other products.

### 7. Acceptable use

Keep the product’s existing necessary rules for lawful use, abuse, security, and the rights of others. Preserve open-source license rights. Alignment is not an opportunity to add broad restrictions on ordinary use or criticism.

### 8. Changes and termination

Give at least 30 days’ advance notice of material terms changes to existing users, unless a shorter period is required for legal or security reasons that are explained. Publish the revision and its effective date, and offer the user a practical opportunity to stop using the affected service. Do not apply a revision retroactively to remove accrued rights. Explain suspension or termination, content access, and recovery proportionately.

For the October 14, 2026 revision, material terms take effect **November 13, 2026 for existing users**, with appropriate advance notice, and **immediately on acceptance for new users**. A product unable to deliver the required notice must not silently claim it has done so. Privacy corrections have a separate publication and effective date of October 14, 2026.

### 9. Warranty and liability

Preserve each product’s existing liability cap and scope. “Nothing in these terms excludes or limits rights or liability that cannot lawfully be excluded or limited, including applicable consumer guarantees.” Do not add new disclaimers or extinguish open-source rights through generic harmonization.

### 10. Governing law and disputes

“Nevada law governs these terms, subject to mandatory laws that protect you where you live. Courts in Clark County, Nevada are the agreed forum, except where applicable consumer law gives you the right to bring a claim in your home courts or requires another forum.” Do not add mandatory arbitration or waive non-waivable rights.

### 11. Contact

Publish the common operator, mailing address, and official legal address above. Keep product support routes available for ordinary help.

## Publication requirements

Before publication, confirm the named operator, reachable official inbox, actual suppliers, relevant retention settings, and accurate local versus hosted claims. Read the rendered pages and their contact and archive links on desktop and mobile. Preserve exact previous text in each site’s native legal history routes; label unknown historical dates honestly. Test consequential behavior such as analytics opt-outs and legal link destinations, rather than snapshotting entire legal documents.

For Studio analytics, `PUBLIC_CLOUDFLARE_WEB_ANALYTICS_TOKEN` enables the manually gated beacon; an unset value loads none. The loader checks DNT and GPC before requesting the script. Keep provider automatic injection disabled. The default `Cache-Control: public, no-transform` response header also prevents Cloudflare’s documented automatic HTML beacon injection; preserve that directive on HTML responses. Review the deployed response and network behavior because a local build cannot establish dashboard settings or production behavior. See [Cloudflare’s setup documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/get-started/) and [RUM documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/observatory/rum-beacon/).

Keep publication receipts, commits, test logs, configuration evidence, and unresolved provider blockers in private build records. They do not belong in evergreen policy prose or task descriptions.
